# Pixelart map rework

The `pixelart` branch is an independent rework of the map's art direction into pixelart, starting with evergreen trees. Keep the world volumetric and lit, with original seeded variants, dimensions and palette shifts. The evergreen reference supplies broad pixel clusters and a tiered silhouette as visual direction, with original generated forms. Deciduous trees and other map props currently retain their existing meshes; the rework is incremental.

`rendering/pixel-evergreens.js` samples species-specific conifer envelopes into small voxel volumes, then merges adjacent exposed faces into indexed rectangles regardless of their colours. Fir has a broad, regularly tiered cone; spruce has a slender spire with hanging needle clusters. Pine uses a crooked, tapering stem and bent limbs supporting overlapping, flattened needle clumps at uneven heights. The open-grown silhouette draws on natural upland Scots pine references from the [Woodland Trust](https://ati.woodlandtrust.org.uk/how-to-record/species-guides/scots-pine/) and [National Trust for Scotland](https://www.nts.org.uk/stories/now-is-the-time-for-pine), rather than regular plantation crowns. Each species has broad/tall forms and eight detail-seed styles, retaining the existing prop position, rotation, scale, overall height, temperature and foliage tone. World generation, dressing descriptors, reservations, locks and export formats are unchanged.

`map-meshes.js` passes the existing forest profile to the tree builder. Named fir, spruce and pine woods use their corresponding models. Evergreen minorities in deciduous woods use the profile's neighbouring species. Taiga selects a repeatable mix of approximately 40% spruce, 40% fir and 20% pine from the existing detail seed; trees outside named woods use an equal mix. Deciduous silhouettes remain unchanged. The thumbnail cache revision invalidates previews of the previous evergreen models.

`pixel-foliage-colors.js` gives each species eight OKLCH swatches with independent hue, chroma and lightness: emerald fir with cool recesses and fresh growth, blue-green/silver spruce, and olive/sage pine with warm tips. The map's element colour contributes a bounded 22% hue tint plus small lightness/chroma adjustments; it no longer determines the whole ramp. Per-prop temperature/tone shifts remain. Bark has separate species colours, including warmer pine upper limbs. Apply night colouring after these species colours, then fog grayscale, so independent colours respect visibility and lighting. Future pixelart props should retain their own material identity with this lighter palette influence.

Stepped needle fans and connected warm/cool patches create deliberate pixel clusters. A shared atlas encodes species and swatch together, keeping all foliage in the same material batches. It uses nearest filtering, no mipmaps and padded patch borders, sorting patches by size to limit texture memory. Its UVs stay attached to the solid crown from every viewing angle. Pixel colour boundaries do not add triangles: reserve geometry for silhouette and relief as the rework expands to other props.

The crowns and bark use rough, lit materials and both cast and receive scene shadows. `pixel-foliage-materials.js` owns palette/temperature/fog atlas variants and bark materials, releasing their textures and materials with the map registry. Time-of-day lighting changes their shading, alongside the existing palette changes, night colouring, fog grayscale and wind deformation. The atlas/template cache holds numeric data only. The trees are volumetric meshes, with normal depth occlusion and geometry-based picking, rather than billboards or image cutouts.

Evergreen geometry retains its UVs and indices through batching. Four-by-four axial groups allow frustum culling and raycast bounds to skip distant forest areas; a conservative bounding-sphere margin includes wind displacement. Face ranges count indexed triangles so picking still resolves the original hex. Other props keep their existing batching behaviour. This reduces the forest cost without changing crown shapes, tree counts or pixel patterns. Fully zoomed-out views still display the entire forest and gain less from culling; additional level-of-detail work can follow measured device results.

The stepped surfaces contain more geometry than the low-poly cones. Review dense forests and physical-phone performance before merging. The approved renderer fixtures still describe the low-poly presentation; refresh those snapshots only after accepting the new look. Keep this experiment off `main` until visual approval.
